The SMS channel sends your messages as SMS to your contacts' phones. Your messages are signed with your **Sender ID**, the name shown as the sender on the phone (for example `ACME`), and go through the same messaging API as the other channels.

Shortext opens SMS in **Cameroon** first, then in other African countries.

## Your Sender ID

The Sender ID is the name your contacts see as the sender of your SMS. You only manage this Sender ID: Shortext takes care of the routes to the mobile operators.

A Sender ID can only send to the customers of an operator (for example MTN or Orange) once **that operator has approved it**. Shortext handles the approvals for you, operator by operator.

Shortext can also give you access to a **shared Sender ID**: a test Sender ID, or a Shortext Sender ID for your announcements. On a shared announcement Sender ID, Shortext reviews the content of your messages before they are sent.

## Send an SMS

SMS uses the same messaging API as the other channels, with a `message` in the [message format](/api-reference/message-format). The SMS channel accepts **text only**:

```json
{
  "type": "text",
  "content": { "text": "Your order A-1042 has left our warehouse." }
}
```

Any other type (template, interactive, media, location, contact) is refused.

## What comes back

- **Delivery receipts**: the status of each SMS is updated as the operator reports it (delivered, or not delivered) and pushed by the `message.status` [webhook](/api-reference/webhooks). Receipts can arrive out of order: Shortext keeps the most advanced status.
- **Replies**: when the operator passes them on, your contacts' replies come into the conversation, like on any channel: the Inbox, the agent and the `message.received` webhook. In many countries, replies to an SMS sent under a Sender ID do not reach the sender: do not rely on them.
- **Unsubscribing**: a contact who answers "Stop" is unsubscribed.
